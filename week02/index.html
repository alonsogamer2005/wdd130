Temple of the Olive Trees

Information about the Temple

The Lima Peru Los Olivos Temple represents a milestone for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints by making the Peruvian capital one of the few cities with two such temples, responding to the growth of a community that exceeds six hundred thousand faithful in the country.

History

Its history formally began on April 3, 2016, when President Thomas S. Monson announced its construction, leading to the groundbreaking ceremony on June 8, 2019, on a site located in San Martín de Porres.

During the years of construction, elements of local identity were integrated, such as Peruvian travertine stone in the interiors, cantuta flowers and four mature olive trees in the exterior gardens.

After the completion of the works, the structure opened its doors to the general public in an open house between November and December of two thousand twenty-three that received more than one hundred thousand visitors,

concluding its development stage on January fourteenth of two thousand twenty-four when Elder D. Todd Christofferson officially dedicated it as the one hundred and eighty-seventh operating temple worldwide.

Architecture

On June 8, 2019, the official groundbreaking ceremony was held, presided over by Elder Enrique R. Falabella. The chosen site covered approximately one hectare in the district of San Martín de Porres (in the Lima Norte area).

During construction, details were incorporated that connect the building to the local identity. Outside, four mature olive trees were planted at the corners of the pedestrian walkways, along with cantuta flowers (Peru's national flower). Inside, it was clad with solid travertine stone extracted directly from Peruvian quarries.

Location

Location: Av. Eloy Espinoza 680 (Urbanización Palao), in the district of San Martín de Porres (border with North Lima).
